Learning Aims & Outcomes

Learning Aims

By the end of this unit, you will be able to introduce yourself and others naturally in English, using nuanced phrases like "It's a pleasure to make your acquaintance" and "May I present my colleague?" This skill is critical for forging professional alliances, expanding social circles, and navigating high-stakes networking events. Mastering introductions enables you to project confidence and cultural awareness in complex English interactions.

Learning Outcomes

  • Employ formal and informal greetings with appropriate register and tone.
  • Elicit and provide detailed information about someone's professional background (e.g., industry, role, expertise).
  • Introduce a third person using sophisticated phrases like "Allow me to introduce..."
  • Respond to introductions with varied expressions such as "Delighted to meet you" or "I've heard so much about you."
  • Analyze and adapt introduction styles based on social versus professional contexts, including cultural nuances.

What We Will Cover

Unit Overview

This unit is structured into two demanding sections that progressively challenge your fluency. First, you will master articulating your own background with nuanced detail, then tackle the complexities of introducing others across varied formal and informal scenarios.

Unit Roadmap

  • Articulating Your Background – Develop the ability to convey detailed personal information—including career history, regional origins, and niche interests—using advanced grammatical structures.
  • Introducing Others – Refine your skill in presenting a friend or colleague with sophisticated phrasing and context-appropriate register for both social and professional environments.

Describing Your Origin and Occupation

Rule / Explanation

When talking about your background, use the verb come from to say where you are from, and work as to describe your job. The structure is: subject + come from + place, and subject + work as + job title. For example, I come from Spain and I work as a teacher. Use the present simple tense because these facts are generally true.

Key Points

  • Come from — Use this phrase to state your place of origin. Example: I come from Italy means Italy is your home country.
  • Work as — Use this to specify your profession. Example: I work as an engineer tells others your job role.
  • Present simple tense — Use this tense for permanent situations like your birthplace and job. No auxiliary verb is needed for positive statements.
  • Preposition 'in' — Use in before a city or country when saying where you work. Example: I work in London.

Examples

✓ I come from Spain — Correct: states the country of origin. ✓ She comes from Mexico — Correct: uses third person singular with -s. ✓ I work as a teacher — Correct: describes the job role. ✓ He works as a doctor — Correct: third person singular with -s. ✓ They come from Brazil — Correct: plural subject with no -s. ✓ We work as nurses — Correct: plural subject with no -s.

Introducing a Friend or Colleague

Rule / Explanation

When introducing someone, you can use various phrases depending on the formality of the situation. In informal settings, This is is common, but in formal contexts, you might use May I introduce or I'd like to present. The introduction should include the person's name, their relationship to you, and optionally a brief description of their role or background. This helps the listener understand who the person is and why they are being introduced.

Key Points

  • Formal vs. Informal — Choose the phrase based on the setting. This is is casual; May I introduce is more formal.
  • Name and Relationship — Always state the person's name and how you know them, e.g., my colleague or our guest speaker.
  • Additional Details — Add a sentence about their role, experience, or a positive trait to give more context.
  • Tone and Body Language — Maintain a confident and welcoming tone. In formal settings, use a more measured pace and avoid overly casual language.

Examples

✓ This is Maria, she's my classmate. — Informal: uses This is + name + description. ✓ May I introduce our keynote speaker, Dr. Elena Rodriguez? — Formal: uses May I introduce + title + name. ✓ I'd like to present my colleague, Mr. Smith, who has been with the company for ten years. — Formal: adds a relative clause for detail. ✓ This is Anna. She works with me. — Informal: adds a second sentence with She works. ✓ Allow me to introduce our special guest, Professor James Whitfield, who is a leading authority in his field. — Formal: uses Allow me to introduce + title + name + description.
